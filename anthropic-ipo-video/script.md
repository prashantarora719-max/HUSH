## 0. Cold open

On Thursday, a company that reported an eighty billion dollar loss in six months is going public.

Investors want it so badly that orders are running at about six times the shares for sale.

The company is Anthropic. It makes an AI model called Claude. And at the top of its price range, it would be worth one point four trillion dollars.

Here is why this matters even if you never buy a single share. Within a few weeks, some of the biggest index funds in America may have to buy it, at whatever price it happens to be trading. One of them could be in your retirement account.

And the biggest winners of this deal are not the people buying on Thursday. They were decided years ago.

## 1. Two quick notes

Two quick notes. This was recorded before the shares started trading, so the final price may differ. And a disclosure. The research for this video was done with the help of Claude, the AI that Anthropic makes. So every number you are about to see has its source on screen. Check them.

## 2. Part one: the deal

Start with the deal itself.

Anthropic is selling eight hundred and twenty-five million shares, at seventy to seventy-eight dollars each. That would raise up to sixty-four billion dollars. Reports call it the second largest stock market debut in history, behind SpaceX earlier this year.

Now, the valuation. You will see two numbers in the headlines, and both are real. Count only the shares that exist today, and Anthropic is worth about one trillion dollars. Count every share that has been promised to employees and investors, and you get one point four trillion.

Either way, it is less than the two trillion that was being whispered a few months ago. Remember that. We will come back to it.

## 3. Part two: the eighty billion dollar loss

Now, that loss.

In the first half of this year, Anthropic brought in sixteen point three billion dollars in revenue. And it reported a net loss of eighty point seven billion.

That sounds like a company on fire. It is not. About seventy-two billion dollars of that loss is an accounting charge. Anthropic had borrowed money using notes that convert into shares. When the company became more valuable, those notes became more expensive on paper. No cash left the building.

Strip that out, and the loss from actually running the business was under seven hundred million dollars. In its most recent reported quarter, Anthropic made an operating profit.

So is it safe? No. Here is the other side.

To run AI, you need computing power. Anthropic spent nearly nine billion dollars on it in six months. That is more than it spent in all of last year. And it has signed contracts to keep spending for years. One analysis of the filing puts its commitment to Google alone above one hundred and twenty billion dollars.

That is the bet. Revenue has to keep growing faster than the bills.

## 4. Part three: the rival that blinked

Which brings us to the company everyone expected to go first. OpenAI. The maker of ChatGPT.

This month, OpenAI went to investors asking for a valuation of one point four trillion dollars. According to reports, investors pushed back. It settled for one point two five trillion. And it raised about fifteen billion dollars, roughly half of what it wanted.

Why the pushback? The Wall Street Journal reported that last quarter, OpenAI took in six point seven billion dollars, and lost twelve point three billion running the business. For every dollar it earned, it lost almost two.

OpenAI's own stock market debut has now been pushed to next year.

So for the first time, the smaller rival may be valued higher than the company that started the boom. That is the story Wall Street is telling this week. Keep in mind who benefits from that story. A company about to sell sixty-four billion dollars of stock.

## 5. Part four: the company that gets paid either way

Now the part most headlines skip. Google.

Google competes with both of these companies. Its own AI, Gemini, has a billion users.

But Google also owns a large piece of Anthropic. Estimates range from about ten to fourteen percent. At the middle of the price range, that stake is worth around one hundred and thirty billion dollars.

And Google sells Anthropic the chips it runs on. Up to a million of them. Google's cloud business more than doubled last quarter, to over thirty billion dollars.

Think about that. If Anthropic wins, Google's shares go up. If Anthropic spends heavily, Google gets the money. And if Anthropic loses, Google still has Gemini.

Amazon is in the same position. It is Anthropic's other giant shareholder, with a stake valued at around one hundred and forty billion dollars, and it sells Anthropic computing power too.

Those are the winners that were decided years ago. They bought in early, and they get paid on the way in and on the way out.

## 6. Part five: who is actually in charge

One more thing the filing tells you. Who is in charge.

Last week we told you how Nvidia's founder could be removed, because he had no special shares. Anthropic is the opposite.

Its seven founders own about thirteen percent of the company. But through a special class of shares, they hold just over half of the votes. Amazon and Google, for all their money, have no vote at all.

And the people buying on Thursday? Together, they get about six percent of the company.

You may think founder control is a good thing or a bad thing. But know that it is the deal. If you own this stock, you are a passenger.

## 7. Part six: your retirement account

So what does this mean for you?

It depends on which fund you own.

Earlier this year, Nasdaq changed its rules. A giant new company can now join the Nasdaq one hundred index after just fifteen trading days. Before, the wait was at least three months. Reuters reports that Anthropic is likely to be added to several major indexes within five to fifteen days.

When that happens, every fund that tracks those indexes has to buy the stock. They do not get to wait for a better price. When SpaceX joined, one estimate put the extra cost to retirement savers at around five hundred million dollars.

So if you own a Nasdaq one hundred fund, or a total market fund, you will probably own a little Anthropic by the end of the year.

But if you own an S and P five hundred fund, you will not. That index kept its old rules. A company must be public for twelve months, and profitable. The earliest Anthropic could join is late next year.

Same company. Same week. Two very different outcomes, depending on a line in your statement that most people have never read.

## 8. Three things to watch

So here is what to watch.

One. The price on Wednesday night. If it lands at the top of the range, or above it, demand is real. If it lands at the bottom, the two trillion dollar whisper was the peak.

Two. Where it closes on Thursday. Above one point two five trillion, and Anthropic is officially worth more than OpenAI.

Three. Mid December. That is when the index decision is due, and when the forced buying would begin.

Every boom has a moment when the public is finally invited in. Sometimes that moment is the beginning. Sometimes it is the top.

The people who built this company, and the giants who funded it, have already been paid.

On Thursday, it is your turn to decide what it is worth.
