# Channel style guide (version 1)

One page. Every video follows it, so the channel looks and feels the same each time.

## The idea in one line
Frank gets overcharged, refuses to let it go, and follows the money until he finds who took it. Every video is a small detective story about a price.

## Cast (fixed, in every video)
| Character | Who | Job in the story | Signature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frank, 58 | Retired, stubborn, remembers what things used to cost | The victim and the detective. Asks what the viewer is thinking | Glasses pushed up on his head, red plaid shirt |
| Danny, 19 | Frank's grandson | The sceptic. Gives the obvious explanation, which turns out to be half the story | Headphones round his neck, grey hoodie, phone |
| The Landlord | Bald, moustache, suit, orange tie | The recurring suspect. Stands for whoever quietly collects the money | Ring of keys, rolled contract |
| Narrator | Calm, dry voice | Tells the facts and teases Frank | Never seen |

## Look
- **Finish:** paper cut-out. Every character and prop looks cut from coloured paper, with a soft shadow beneath it, like a pop-up book.
- **Drawing:** flat colour, thick dark navy outline (#1a2240), rounded simple shapes, dot eyes. No shading, no gradients on characters.
- **Palette:** red #e8372c, yellow #ffc928, navy #243a8f, cream #fff3d6, plus one accent colour taken from the brand in that video.
- **Fonts:** Anton for headlines and numbers, Bangers for speech bubbles. White or yellow letters with a navy outline.
- **Numbers live in the world:** on menu boards, tills, price tags, receipts, signs. A separate chart only when nothing else can show it, and never for more than 6 seconds.
- **No real logos or mascots.** Company names in text only.

## Shots
- A new picture every 2 to 4 seconds. Nothing stays still for more than 6.
- Pattern for each beat: wide (where are we), close (who reacts), detail (the thing itself).
- Every spoken line gets its own action: a pose change, a prop arriving, a number moving.
- Camera: a small punch-in on each cut, a slow push during the shot, a shake on big numbers.
- Characters always blink, bounce a little when speaking, and land with a squash when they change pose.
- On-screen text: four words at most.

## Sound
- A pop for everything that appears, a whoosh on each cut, a till ring on money, a thud on big numbers.
- Music is light and playful with a steady groove. Never tense, never alarming.
- The music stops for the joke or the reveal, then comes back.

## Script
1. **First 5 seconds:** start inside a scene with a price and a reaction. No introduction, no "in this video".
2. **By 30 seconds:** one clear question the viewer wants answered, and a suspect.
3. **Every 60 to 90 seconds:** a suspect is cleared and a new one appears. The answer is never given early.
4. **One joke a minute,** usually Frank against Danny or the narrator against Frank.
5. **The reveal** arrives in the last third and is something most viewers did not know.
6. **Ending:** Frank's verdict in one line, then what it means for the viewer's own wallet.
7. **Facts:** every number has a source in the fact sheet. Estimates are labelled. No invented drama.
8. **Length:** 8 to 10 minutes.

## Thumbnail and title
- Frank's face large, one clear emotion, looking at one object (receipt, burger, price tag).
- Three words or fewer, in Anton, readable at phone size.
- Red or yellow background, high contrast, nothing small.
- Title names the brand or product and promises the answer. The thumbnail shows the feeling; it does not repeat the title.
- Made and agreed before the script is written.

## Review
- Retention checked 48 hours after each upload: where viewers leave tells us which rule to tighten.
- After 12 videos: keep the format if one passes 10,000 views with over 35% watched; rethink if none passes 2,000.
