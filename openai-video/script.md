# OpenAI: Paper Trillions, Entry No. 2 (draft script)

**Title options**
1. OpenAI Is Worth $1.25 Trillion. It Loses $2 for Every $1 It Makes.
2. Where Does OpenAI's Money Actually Go?
3. The $1.25 Trillion Company That Can't Stop Losing Money

**Thumbnail idea:** "LOSES $2 FOR EVERY $1" on paper, with a red circle around the $2.
**Audience:** United States, 30 to 50, curious about money and tech, not working in AI
**Length:** about 920 spoken words, roughly 7 minutes at the last video's pace. That is under YouTube's 8-minute line for mid-roll ads, so it needs about 300 more words before recording.
**Status:** DRAFT. Figures are from one round of research on November 11, 2026 and are not yet checked against the original reports. See the fact sheet at the end.

OpenAI is a private company, so almost every number here is "as reported", not audited. The script says so out loud more than once; keep those lines.

Text in *[brackets]* is a note, not narration.

---

## 0. Cold open

This company is worth one and a quarter trillion dollars.

On paper.

Last quarter, it brought in six point seven billion dollars. And lost twelve point three.

For every dollar it earns, it loses almost two.

It has promised to spend more than eight hundred billion dollars on computing power. Money it does not have yet.

And of every twenty people who use it, nineteen pay nothing.

This is OpenAI. And this is where the money goes.

## 1. The hole is getting deeper

One thing first. OpenAI is a private company. It does not publish its accounts. Every number in this video comes from figures it has shown investors, as reported by outlets like the Wall Street Journal and the Financial Times.

And those figures tell a strange story.

From the first quarter of this year to the second, OpenAI's revenue grew eighteen percent. Good. But its losses grew by about a third.

Most businesses lose less as they grow. This one loses more.

So where is it all going? Four places. And the fourth is the one that should worry you, because it involves a number from OpenAI's own plan. We will get to it.

## 2. Where it goes, one: the free users

Start with the product you already know. ChatGPT.

About nine hundred million people use it every week. That is more than one in ten humans on earth.

Around fifty million of them pay.

So for every person with a subscription, there are about nineteen using it for free. And unlike a free video or a free search, every single answer costs real money to produce. A chip has to run. Electricity has to be bought.

That is the first leak. The more popular it gets, the more it costs.

## 3. Where it goes, two: the promises

The second place is bigger. Much bigger.

To keep up with demand, OpenAI has been signing deals for computing power. Not for today. For years from now.

One deal alone, with Oracle, is reported at three hundred billion dollars. Sixty billion a year, starting in 2027.

*[beat]*

Think about that. Sixty billion a year, to one supplier. OpenAI's revenue for the entire first half of this year was about twelve.

Add up all the deals, and the estimates for what it will spend on computing by 2030 run from six hundred billion dollars to more than eight hundred and fifty billion.

Those are promises. The money to keep them has not been raised yet.

## 4. Where it goes, three: the race

Why promise so much? Because of the third place the money goes. The race.

Every few months, someone releases a better model. To stay in front, you have to train a bigger one. And each one costs more than the last.

And OpenAI's lead is shrinking. A year ago, by one traffic measure, ChatGPT had about four out of every five visits to AI chatbots. This spring it was closer to one in two. Google's Gemini took much of the rest.

It is still the biggest. But it is paying more every year to stay there.

## 5. So who is paying for all this?

*[re-hook, about the 5-minute mark]*

If you lose two dollars for every one you make, somebody has to cover the difference. So who is it?

Investors. In March, OpenAI raised a hundred and twenty-two billion dollars, the largest private funding round in history. Eight months later, it is raising again, at that one and a quarter trillion dollar valuation.

And some of the money comes from the companies it buys from. Nvidia, which sells it chips, has also invested in it. If you watched our last video, you know that pattern. We called it the money loop.

So the losses are real. But as long as investors keep writing checks, they do not matter.

*[beat]*

Which raises the obvious question. Why would anyone keep writing them?

## 6. The case for OpenAI

Here is the honest answer. Because the other side of the ledger is extraordinary too.

Last year, OpenAI's revenue was reported at around thirteen billion dollars. By this September, it was running at close to fifty billion a year. Almost no company in history has grown that fast.

In February, it started showing ads. Within about two hundred days, that was reportedly a billion-dollar-a-year business.

And remember those nineteen free users? Google and Meta built two of the most profitable companies on earth out of people who pay nothing.

Investors are not betting on what OpenAI earns today. They are betting it becomes the next Google. If they are right, one and a quarter trillion dollars will look cheap.

## 7. Where it goes, four: the number in the plan

Now, the number I promised you.

According to a Financial Times report on OpenAI's own projections, the company expects to burn through two hundred and seventy-eight billion dollars between now and 2030.

*[slow]*

That is not the pessimistic case. That is the plan. That is what happens if everything goes right.

No company has ever needed that much outside money to reach profit. Not Amazon. Not Uber. Not close.

## 8. What has to go right

So here is the ledger, as honestly as I can write it.

For the paper trillion to become real, three things have to happen.

One. Investors have to keep funding it, year after year, through a quarter of a trillion dollars of losses.

Two. The cost of each answer has to fall faster than the number of answers grows.

Three. Those free users have to turn into money, through subscriptions or through ads.

Any one of those is possible. It needs all three.

And here is why it matters to you. OpenAI is aiming to sell shares to the public in 2027. When that happens, this stops being a problem for venture capitalists. It could land in your index fund.

One and a quarter trillion dollars.

On paper.

*[end]*

## How this keeps people watching without misleading them
- **A promise made early and kept:** section 1 says a fourth place is coming with "a number from OpenAI's own plan"; section 7 delivers it.
- **A count:** "four places" means viewers always know how many are left.
- **A real counter-argument:** section 6 gives the strongest case for OpenAI. That is fairer, and viewers stay to see which side wins.
- **A question at the midpoint:** "So who is paying for all this?" arrives where attention usually dips.
- **A personal stake at the end:** the 2027 share sale and your index fund.
- **Nothing overstated:** every figure is labelled as reported, and the ending says it could work.
