**Format:** one true story, told as a thriller. Narrator only (voice: Titan). 12 to 15 minutes, about 2,000 to 2,300 words.
**Status:** outline for approval. Every fact marked [CHECK] is unconfirmed in `fact-sheet.md` and will be verified or cut before the script is written.

## Cold open (0:00 to 0:50), written
> November, 1963. A man with a flashlight climbs to the top of a storage tank in Bayonne, New Jersey.
> On paper, this tank holds millions of pounds of salad oil. Banks have lent a fortune against it. American Express has put its name on it.
> He opens the hatch and lowers a measuring line. It comes up coated in oil. For years, that has been enough.
> This time, he lets the line go deeper.
> And it comes up wet. With water.
> There were dozens of tanks like it. On paper they held more oil than existed in the entire United States. In four days, two Wall Street firms would be gone, American Express would be fighting for its life, and the man who built it all, a butcher's assistant from the Bronx, would be the most wanted name in American business.
> This is how he did it. And why nobody looked down.

The inspector scene is a reconstruction of how the tanks were finally checked, and will be labelled as one. Whether the discovery happened in exactly this way is [CHECK].

## How it will look
- **Period documents:** newspaper front pages, court and exchange notices, photographs and maps, used where they are public domain or clearly licensed, with slow moves and highlighted lines. Source and date shown on screen.
- **Illustrated reconstructions:** one consistent painted style for scenes no camera recorded (the tank farm at night, the trading floor, the back office), each labelled "reconstruction".
- **Built graphics:** the cutaway tank filling with water, the pipe maze, receipts multiplying across a map, the price line collapsing, a four-day calendar counting down.
- **No cartoon characters.** Real people appear only in genuine photographs or as unidentifiable figures in illustrations.

## How it will sound
- A score in three moods: uneasy calm for the build, a rising pulse for the bet, release after the collapse.
- Atmosphere under scenes: harbour at night, ticker tape, telephones, a trading floor.
- A hit or a silence on each reveal. The longest silence is the measuring line coming up wet.
