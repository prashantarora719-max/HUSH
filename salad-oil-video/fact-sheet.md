# The salad oil swindle (1963): fact sheet, first pass

Compiled November 21, 2026 from search summaries of the pages linked below. I could not open the pages themselves from this environment, so every row needs a second check against the page or the book before recording. Where sources disagree, both versions are listed.

**The definitive source** is Norman C. Miller, *The Great Salad Oil Swindle* (1965). Miller covered the case for the Wall Street Journal and won a Pulitzer Prize for it in 1964. I have not read the book; several scene details below should be confirmed in it.

## The gamble and the collapse
| Claim | Detail | Confidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| The corner | From 1962 he bought soybean oil and cottonseed oil futures on a huge scale. By August 1963 he reportedly held 79% of cottonseed oil contracts on the Produce Exchange and 20% of soybean oil contracts in Chicago | Medium | [Shortform](https://www.shortform.com/blog/salad-oil-scandal/), Wikipedia |
| Why | Prices were rising on talk of a US trade deal with the Soviet Union | Low to medium | [MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/investments/investment-strategy/601677/great-frauds-in-history-tino-de-angelis-salad-oil-scandal) |
| The trigger | Mid-November 1963: the deal talk collapsed and prices fell sharply over two days | Low: one podcast description | To verify |
| Bankruptcy | Allied filed on November 19, 1963; the news broke publicly on November 20 | High | Wikipedia |
| The brokers | The New York Stock Exchange suspended Ira Haupt & Co. and Williston & Beane. Haupt was owed at least $18 million, Williston & Beane $1.61 million | Medium | [Time, 1965](https://time.com/archive/6833300/crime-the-man-who-fooled-everybody) |
| November 22 | Williston & Beane was rescued and reopened at noon. President Kennedy was shot that afternoon; the exchange halted trading at 2:07 pm and stayed shut until November 26 | Medium | [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/government/21/05/21218856/wall-street-crime-and-punishment-the-salad-oil-scandal-of-1963) |

| Total losses | About $175 million to $180 million across roughly 51 lenders | Medium | Wikipedia, MoneyWeek |

## Handle with care
- Do not tie the Kennedy assassination to the fraud beyond the timeline: the two only overlapped by date.
- "Seawater" versus "water": use "water" unless Miller confirms seawater.
- American Express is a living company: describe 1963 only, from published accounts.

### Corrections to the first pass
- The fraud was exposed by the futures collapse and a creditor finding empty tanks, not by an inspector's measuring line. The cold open is rewritten.
- Only Ira Haupt collapsed. Williston & Beane was suspended, rescued and later merged. The script says "a Wall Street firm... was dead".
- "Seawater" is supported: the book is quoted as saying salt water.
