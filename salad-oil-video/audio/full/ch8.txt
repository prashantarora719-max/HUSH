[quiet, final] When the tanks were opened, Tino was not there. He was found days later. Investigators traced half a million dollars to a Swiss bank account, which he handed back.

In January nineteen sixty-five he pleaded guilty to four federal charges. He was out of prison by nineteen seventy-two.  Within a few years he was accused of another swindle, this time involving hogs. 

The reporter who unravelled the story, Norman Miller of the Wall Street Journal, won a Pulitzer Prize.

Tino never accepted the verdict. "No one in the world," he said, "ever did more for the economy of this country than I did."

Six years. Fifty-one lenders. One of the most trusted names in America.

All of them could have known. All it took was one man, one line, and the patience to let it sink past the first two feet.
