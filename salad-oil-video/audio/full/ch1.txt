[warm, storytelling] His name was Anthony De Angelis. Everyone called him Tino.

He was born in the Bronx in nineteen fifteen, the son of Italian immigrants, and he went to work young, in a meat and fish market. By nineteen thirty-eight he had his own hog-cutting business, and he was good at it.

He was also, from the start, a man who cut corners.

After the war he sold lard to Yugoslavia. The Yugoslav government sued, saying the product was substandard. He settled.

In nineteen forty-nine he took control of a New Jersey meat packer called Adolph Gobel and won a contract to supply the federal school lunch programme. The government then accused the company of overstating weights and shipping two million pounds of uninspected meat. He settled that too.

By nineteen fifty-three Gobel was bankrupt, and Tino was out as president. Investigators concluded he had been borrowing money against inventory that did not exist.

Remember that detail. Borrowing against goods that aren't there. In nineteen fifty-three it cost him one company.

Two years later, he tried it again. This time, with someone respectable to vouch for him.
