[measured, reflective] Legally, the receipts were issued by a subsidiary. By the end of December it was bankrupt, facing two hundred and ten million dollars in claims. Its insurers refused to pay for fraud. American Express could have walked away. 

But American Express did not really sell travel. It sold trust. That year it issued five billion dollars in travellers cheques and money orders. If its name on a piece of paper could be worthless, why would anyone hold the others?

Its shares fell by a third.

The company's president, Howard Clark, told shareholders that American Express felt "morally bound" to see the losses made good. In time, it offered the creditors sixty million dollars.

Most of Wall Street saw a company in trouble. A thirty-three-year-old investor in Omaha saw one whose customers had not gone anywhere. Warren Buffett bought about five percent of American Express while others were selling. 

It became one of the most famous investments of his life.
